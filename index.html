Who we are

Our story

The International Foundation for Sexual Health Education (IFSHE) was set up in 2018. Its founding members saw a gap in sexual health training and, drawing on their experience in medical education, formed a voluntary group of professionals working in sexual health and HIV. Our work began with a sexually transmitted infections foundation course in Sudan, with SHAWG, in 2023.

Since then we have developed our own material and training model with local and international partners. Each programme is tailored to the cultural, legal and healthcare context of the region it serves, while keeping high standards of clinical accuracy and teaching. In 2026 we became the International Foundation for Sexual Health and HIV Education (IFSHHE), to reflect our broader focus on HIV alongside sexual health.

At the heart of our work are local colleagues and organisations who share our vision and bring priceless knowledge, skills and sensitivities. We put local ownership first, through train-the-trainer models, cost-effective delivery and respectful engagement with local context and values.

Designed with local colleagues, not for them.

What we do

Our vision

To promote, educate and improve sexual health and HIV care with healthcare professionals around the world, merging medical expertise with cultural insight, together with local colleagues.

01

Sustainable education and training

High-quality programmes that bridge theory and practice, and pathology and service delivery, building capacity that keeps having an impact long after our direct involvement ends.

02

Capacity and partnerships

Strong partnerships with local organisations and professionals, so training is culturally relevant, accessible and aligned with local healthcare needs.

03

Empowering local professionals

Train-the-trainer models, access to educational resources and best practice, and support for local experts to continue and expand training.

How we work

Our activities

Bespoke education and training for nurses, doctors, community workers and allied health professionals — from webinars and workshops to full courses — tailored to local healthcare systems and delivered by volunteer IFSHHE faculty alongside local colleague facilitators.

We cover the full range of sexual health and HIV care, from epidemiology, assessment, diagnosis and management to partner notification, prevention, health promotion and key populations. Specialist areas include STIs in pregnancy, gender-based violence, sexual dysfunction, dermatology, and service design and delivery.

  1. 1

    Needs assessment

    We start with a needs assessment, so each programme fits its participants and the local health system.

  2. 2

    Peer-reviewed material

    Teaching material is peer reviewed and refined with local faculty before delivery.

  3. 3

    Interactive delivery

    Lectures, workshops and pre-course materials, with local facilitators who go on to train others.

  4. 4

    Reflect and refine

    Pre- and post-course tests, daily reflections, faculty debriefs and evaluation shape what comes next.

SD

Sudan

Partner · SHAWG · Sudan Ministry of Health

Year · February–March 2023

Our inaugural course: a three-day foundation course in sexually transmitted infections for healthcare professionals in Khartoum, delivered with the Sudan HIV/AIDS Working Group (SHAWG) and endorsed by the Sudan Ministry of Health.

What we did

  • Taught 42 healthcare professionals at the Omdurman HIV/AIDS Antiretroviral Therapy Centre
  • Adapted the UK STI Foundation course for Sudan, adding HIV, hepatitis, sexual dysfunction and TB, with local speakers
  • Lectures, small-group work, role play and practical sessions shaped to the local cultural and religious context
  • Supported by the IOM Health Development Programme, the Patients Helping Fund and the WHO

What it led to Participants rated the teaching 4–5 out of 5 and asked for more. Conflict has since halted further work in Sudan, but we stay closely connected with SHAWG and Sudanese colleagues and hope to resume when conditions permit.

TZ

Tanzania

Partner · RDTC, Moshi · ILDS · IFD

Year · February 2026

The first dedicated, interactive short course in sexual health care designed for resource-limited settings, delivered with the Regional Dermatology Training Centre in Moshi and supported by the ILDS and the International Foundation for Dermatology.

What we did

  • Lectures, small-group case discussions and practical sessions for RDTC students and staff
  • Sexual history taking, STI management, syphilis, testicular and pelvic examination, and STIs and HIV in pregnancy
  • RDTC's own faculty co-taught sessions, so the course can be repeated locally
  • Daily reflections and participant feedback shaped each day

What it led to A course that sits inside an established regional training centre and can be run again by local faculty. Talks are under way to support the development of an STI service.

Our impact

Measured by what lasts

We measure our impact through participant feedback, knowledge assessments and ongoing engagement with local partners. We collect data on participant demographics, learning needs and course effectiveness to keep refining our programmes, with cost-effectiveness and sustainability always in mind.

  • Participant feedback

    Daily reflections and post-course evaluations from participants.

  • Knowledge assessments

    Pre- and post-course tests show what participants have learned.

  • Ongoing engagement

    Regular contact with local partners and faculty during and after each course.

  • Local ownership

    Local healthcare professionals equipped to deliver ongoing education themselves.
